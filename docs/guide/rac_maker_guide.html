RAC Maker

Build your own Ratchet & Clank planet, then play it with your controller.

Start here

The editor is the game itself, paused. What you see is exactly what you will play.

  • The tool strip (the left edge) holds every tool, keys 1 to 0: Select Objects Islands Shape Edges Sculpt Paths Rails Logic World.
  • The left panel shows the tool's own things under its name: the objects to place, the brushes, the zone kinds, the sky. Select shows the controls and On this map, a list of everything on the map by kind.
  • The right panel shows whatever is selected, in any tool: an object, an island, a path, a rail, a zone, a lamp. With nothing selected it shows the map: start, death height, ship, loadout, floors. The Map chip at its top (or Esc) goes back there; the bin deletes the selection.
  • The top bar: New, Save, Undo, Redo, Play (F5), the map's picture and the collision view; on the right the radio, the views (the eye), the fly speed and the help (F1).
  • The bottom bar shows what the mouse buttons and keys do in the tool you hold.
Every change appears straight away and reloads the level in the background in about half a second, so what you build is always playable.

Camera

  • Hold the right mouse button and move the mouse to look around.
  • W A S D fly, Space up, Q down. Hold Shift to go five times faster.
  • The mouse wheel (over the world, not a panel) changes the flying speed. The top bar shows the speed; click it to put it back to 16.
If the camera ever feels slow, you scrolled the wheel down over the world. Scroll up or press the reset.

Placing objects

  • In Objects, filter by type (Enemies & creatures, Crates & pickups, Platforms & machines, Scenery, Effects) and by planet. Live only hides objects that do not work away from their home planet yet.
  • Click an object in the list, then click in the world. A green box shows where it will land: on the ground, on top of an object you point at its top, or next to it when you point at its side.
  • Press R (or Shift+R) while placing to turn it first. Esc stops placing.
  • Select an object to see it on the right: position, facing, and Add another on top, left, right, in front or behind, which is great for rows and stacks of crates.
  • Crates that touch are linked automatically: break the bottom crate and the ones above fall, as in the game.
  • Solid (on the right) builds collision from an object's shape. Many pieces had no collision of their own in the game (the planet around them was solid), so walls, rocks and other scenery get it automatically. Leave it off for things that move or break: the collision stays where you put it. An object with collision of its own says Solid by itself; its Extra solid from its model adds collision from its whole shape on top, which stays where it stands even if the object moves or breaks.
  • A red line from an object means the game moves it somewhere else when the level loads, using data from its home planet. Such objects cannot stay where you put them yet.
Some objects follow paths, trigger boxes or cameras from their home planet. Until paths come to the editor they may stand still or move strangely.

Moving & turning

  • Drag a selected object to slide it along the ground. It stops against other objects instead of going inside them.
  • R / Shift+R turn it 15°. Delete removes it.
  • Exact numbers can be typed on the right (click a number, type, Enter).

Terrain

  • The terrain tools are in the tool strip: Islands (build), Shape, Edges (openings), Sculpt, Paths and Rails. Esc puts down whatever you hold, lets go of the selection, then comes back to Select. Click an island, a path or a rail to see its settings on the right: ground, colour, edge, surface, size and place, roof.
  • Texture and plant grids show one planet at a time: step through the planets with the arrows by its globe.
  • A new map starts on a big flat patch of grass: it is terrain like any other, so you can sculpt it, reshape it or delete it.
  • Build islands: choose a shape (Round, Square, Blob or Draw), how far across, its height above the ground and its cliff depth (walls down from the edge, closed underneath). A green outline follows the mouse and shows exactly where the island will go: its top, its cliffs and the spot on the ground below. Click to build it, on the ground or out in the air. Drag instead of clicking to pull the island out from where you press to the size you want.
  • Draw (the fourth shape): hold the left mouse button and trace the coast in the world, then let go. The island gets exactly the shape you drew, at the height above the ground you set.
  • The texture under New island's ground is for the next island you build. To change an island that is already there, select it: its own texture is in the right panel.
  • Edge: pick from Railings (the Aridia truss railing, the Kerwan iron fence, the Pokitaru railing and more, shown as pictures) or Walls (stone, rock, metal and concrete walls from the game, or a Textured wall of any height up to 30 with the texture you choose). It is laid all the way round the island's coast. Invisible wall blocks without anything drawn. Blocks up to sets how high Ratchet cannot get past it. Right after you build an island, changing the edge, texture or cliff depth in Build islands also changes that island; for any island, select it and use the right panel.
  • Openings: click an island, then click a stretch of its edge to take it out (it turns red): a gap to walk through or jump from. Click a red opening to put the edge back, or hold the button and run along the edge to do many at once. Openings stay where you put them when the coast moves; Close all puts the whole edge back. The Edge gaps brush in Sculpt takes railing out (or, with Shift, puts it back) wherever you brush. Ratchet stops on the land side of a railing, and walks through every opening you see.
  • Surface (right panel, and for each path): Ground, Slippery (ice: Ratchet slides), Hot (burns: he bounces off hurt), Deadly (touching it costs a life) or Magnetic (with the Magneboots he walks up walls and across ceilings). They work on every host planet.
  • Paths: walkways and bridges. Click where a path starts, then where it bends, and press Enter when done. Click from one island to another and it is a bridge across the air; the railings it crosses open by themselves (close them again in Openings). Drag a point to move it, turn the mouse wheel over a point to raise or lower it, Delete over a point removes it. Each path has a width, a thickness, a texture, a surface and an edge (railings or walls on both sides).
  • Shade brush (Sculpt): tints the ground with a colour (shadow, dark, warm, moss, burnt, snow or any colour you pick): shadows under trees, scorched or mossy patches. With Shift it clears the tint.
  • Decor brush (Sculpt): pick a plant, rock or detail of the planet (Objects > Planet chooses which) and drag to strew it over the land, each turned and sized a little differently. With Shift it picks them up. They stay on the ground when you sculpt or move the island.
  • Collision (top bar, or F6, also while playing): shows what Ratchet stands on and bumps into near the camera: green ground, blue slippery, orange hot, red deadly, purple magnetic, grey cliffs, yellow edges. When you save, the editor checks the map; a warning sign next to Save lists what it found: holes in the collision, a start over nothing, ground below the death height.
  • Roof (right panel): a flat textured plane over the island's land at the height you choose, seen from above and below. Ratchet can stand on it. A tall textured wall with a roof makes a room or a cave.
  • Shape: click an island to select it. White dots appear along its coast: drag a dot out to grow the land that way, drag it in to cut a bay. The yellow circle is the dot's reach, how much coast follows it: turn the mouse wheel over a dot to change it (small for a sharp point, big for a whole side). The island's grid grows by itself when you pull past it.
  • In Shape, the green diamond in the middle moves the whole island and the blue arrow raises or lowers it. Grow all and Shrink all move the whole coast by a unit. Hold Ctrl to snap to the grid. Each drag is one Ctrl+Z.
  • Sculpt: choose a brush and drag on the land. Hold Shift to do the opposite. Radius and strength change the brush; Square makes it square. The brush's outline lies on the ground, over hills and dips.
  • Raise / Lower make hills and valleys. Plateau lifts the ground to one flat height, perfect for platforms and ledges (with Shift: flat-bottomed pits). Smooth softens, Flatten levels everything to where you started the stroke.
  • Paint: pick a texture in the brush's texture grid and drag to paint it over the patch, blending softly into the rest.
  • Land: paint land on or off by hand (with Shift: cut holes and gaps). Cliffs and the edge follow the new shape.
  • On the right, set the selected island's texture, its texture size (how often it repeats), its cliff depth and its edge.
  • The list of patches lets you select and delete them. Ratchet can walk on everything you build, and the edges stop him like walls.
  • Rail points: pick one in the world or in the rail's point list to move it with its arrows (x, y, up) or type its numbers. Rails go both ways unless One way is ticked.
  • Islands pushed into each other (at about the same height) lose their railing where they meet.
  • A path's Stop where it meets land (on for new paths) builds it only over the air: a bridge from coast to coast, with nothing poking through the islands.
  • Rails: grind rails. Look picks a planet's grind rail (Kerwan cable, Batalia, Oltanis, Kalebo, Veldin) repeated along it, or a plain beam. Click where the rail starts, then where it bends, and press Enter. Smooth turns the corners into curves; Lift sets how high above the ground new points go, and the mouse wheel over a point raises or lowers it. Drag points to move them, Delete over a point removes it. Grind rails are switched on for the map by themselves (map settings, Grind rails); Ratchet needs the Grindboots in the loadout.
Island chain: build three islands at different heights with railings, open the railing where a bridge lands (Openings), then draw a path from island to island. The example map three_islands.json is built that way.

Logic: zones & rules

  • The Logic tool places zones: boxes in the world that do something when Ratchet walks into them. Pick a kind, then click in the world. Click a zone to select it and drag it to move it; on the right set its name, width, length, height, turn and where its bottom is.
  • Checkpoint (blue): when Ratchet walks in, the game saves his place. After a death he comes back there.
  • Goal (yellow): walking in finishes the map. A banner shows your time, the jingle plays, and a few seconds later you are back in the editor.
  • Death zone (red): walking in costs a life. Use it for pits, lava or anything the death height does not cover.
  • Camera (purple): while Ratchet is inside, the camera stands at the zone's purple ball and watches him, and it blends back when he leaves. Fly the editor camera to where it should stand and press Use the current view as its camera.
  • Trigger (green): does nothing alone; rules watch it.
  • Rules: when something happens, then things happen. When: Ratchet enters a zone, all enemies beaten in a zone (the enemies standing in it when the map starts), or an object is destroyed. Then: remove an object, show an object, a message on the screen, a checkpoint at a zone, or the goal. Each rule runs once per play.
  • To use an object in a rule, give it a Name in its panel. Tick Hidden until a rule shows it for an ambush or a bridge that appears: until then it is not there, not solid and does not move.
  • Show it with the camera (on a rule that removes or shows an object): the camera turns to the object, it disappears or appears, then the view cuts back to Ratchet.
  • In a camera zone the stick follows that camera: push it towards where Ratchet should go on screen. Grinding keeps the usual controls.
  • Saving warns about rules that name something not on the map, names used twice and hidden objects that no rule shows.
An arena: a Trigger zone over an island with two robot troopers in it, a Reinforced crate named Gate on the bridge out, and the rule when all enemies beaten in Arena then remove Gate, message "The gate is open!". The example map Sample course has one, plus an ambush, a grind rail with a camera zone and a goal.

Enemies

  • Select an enemy to see its sight: the blue circle is where it notices Ratchet, the red circle where it attacks. Shown for about forty enemies whose ranges are known.
  • Robot troopers and the Veldin tank take a path (right panel): Square patrol or 8 ahead, then drag the points in the world. Troopers patrol it; the tank drives from point to point in a random order. A trooper without a path guards where it stands.
  • Enemies bring what they need from their planet: their guns, shots, treads and riders, their patrol areas and trigger boxes, turned and moved with them. A few still need companions or their planet's script and are marked may vanish on maps; set pieces (bosses, races, space fights) cannot be placed.
  • Enemies marked may vanish on maps in the object list still lean on their home planet's data: they can disappear or wander off. Try them with Play before building around them.
  • The objects are sorted into categories: Enemies, Bosses, Creatures, Characters, Machines (the vendor, teleporters, kiosks), Crates, Pickups, Gadget points, Platforms & lifts, Doors & gates, Switches & triggers, Hazards, Breakables, Vehicles, Scenery and Effects. The search finds names, categories and moby ids. Pieces, shots and parts the game makes itself are left out.
  • Breakables holds only what Ratchet can break; its panel says what breaks it (any hit, or explosions only). Walls only a boss, a story shot or Giant Clank breaks are with the scenery, and say so.
  • An object's panel shows its category and its file (for example rac1/L01/moby/729). Amber notes say what it needs on a map: its partner (a switch, a crank), its planet's route, a group, or that it is a story object.
  • Breakables have Hides: put a gold bolt or a crate behind one, hidden until it breaks (a rule in Logic does it).
  • Objects that move, break or have their own collision are never baked solid, so a wall you blow up is gone, collision and all. Each gold bolt on a map is its own (collecting one no longer takes the others).
  • Ammo crates refill the weapons Ratchet owns: tick a weapon under Loadout in the map settings, or the crate gives nothing (saving warns about it). Bolt and Nanotech crates always work.

World: sky, song, ship

  • The World tool sets the sky and light: pick another planet and the map gets its sky, background colour and fog. Own fog sets a fog colour, where it starts and where it is full.
  • Raise and turn (under the sky tiles) move the sky up or down and spin it round.
  • Planet: the planet Ratchet's ship flies to when you press Play, as the game flies between its planets; your map's name shows under it. Start from a look (Lush, Desert, Ice, Lava, Gas giant, Toxic), then pick its pattern (land & sea, bands, craters, swirls), its three colours, the glow of its air, clouds and a ring. New world gives another layout of land and sea. Untick Fly here on Play to start playing at once.
  • Themes: a whole look in one click (sky, fog, light, colours and song): Toxic, Frozen, Volcanic, Night City, Desert Noon, Alien Dusk and Ruins. Change any part after.
  • Light: your own sun (its direction, height and colour), the ambient (the light in the shade) and shade hollows (dips and the feet of slopes darker) for your islands, paths and pieces. The game's objects keep their own light.
  • Colours: shift the whole map's hue, saturation and brightness and mix in a tint. Objects too also recolours the enemies, crates, pickups and Ratchet; leave it off to keep them easy to see.
  • The fog now also covers your own terrain, paths and pieces, as it covers the game's.
  • One ground texture, everywhere it is used (in Colours): tick a texture of your map to shift its hue, saturation, brightness and tint on its own, on every island and path that uses it.
  • Lights: Add a light where the camera looks, then set its colour, reach, strength and place. Lights shine on your ground and on the objects near them (the first four).
  • Sky layers (under the sky tiles): a sky is drawn in layers (a dome, clouds, a sun, mountains); hide or tint each.
  • An object's panel has Tint: one object coloured on its own.
  • An island's or a path's panel has Colour: Tint this island (or path) colours just it; Its texture's colours, everywhere shifts its texture on every island and path that uses it.
  • Changes in the World tool show at once (light, colours, lights, fog). The sky and the objects' colours change when you let go of the slider.
  • Some skies move on their own: twinkling and shooting stars (Veldin, Aridia, Gemlik Base, Blarg Station, Drek's Fleet and others) and cloud layers that slowly turn. Under the sky tiles, Stars and shooting stars and Clouds drift switch them off.
  • Saving warns when Objects too shifts the enemies' and pickups' colours hard, or when the fog is so thick that things disappear in it.
  • Song: Use picks what the map plays (any planet's themes; the host's song or Silence also work). Press ▶ next to a song to listen while you edit (the radio) and ■ to stop it.
  • Ratchet's ship (map settings, Start: Ship): the level starts with the ship flying in and landing, as on the game's planets. The ship is locked until the goal is reached ("Back to your ship!"); then walk to its hatch and press △ to board, fly off and finish ("Level complete"). With a ship, Ratchet starts by its hatch (the blue circle follows the ship): make sure there is ground there and a way to the ship.

Playing your map

  • Press Play or F5. Ratchet starts at the start point (the blue circle), with the loadout you ticked in the map settings.
  • Play first flies Ratchet's ship through space to your planet (World > Planet), shows its name, then lands (with a ship) or starts at once. Any button skips the flight.
  • Press F5 again to stop. Everything you broke or killed comes back, and the camera returns to where you were.
  • Set the start point, death height, rails and loadout in the right panel with nothing selected.
  • Reaching a Goal zone shows your time; a few seconds later you are back in the editor.

Maps, saving & backups

  • Ctrl+S saves. A dot after the map's name means unsaved changes.
  • New starts a fresh map in your maps folder (RAC Maker in your Documents folder; maps made before in C:\Users\you\Documents\RAC Maker stay there).
  • Recent (main menu, left) lists the maps you opened last, also ones outside the maps folder.
  • Share (main menu, with a map picked) saves it as one .racmap file with its picture, in the maps folder's Shared folder, and shows it in Explorer: send that file. A map holds only your work and references to the game's parts, so the other player needs their own extracted game, as for ReRAC.
  • Import… (main menu, next to Your maps) lists the shared maps in your Downloads and on your Desktop; Browse… picks any file in Windows' file window. The ReRAC Launcher's RAC Maker tile also imports, plays and edits maps.
  • The main menu lists every map in the folder: click one to open it.
  • Every save keeps a backup of the version it replaces, and unsaved work is backed up every 5 minutes. Open any backup from the main menu (click the map).
  • Drag a card in the main menu to put your maps in the order you like.
  • The main menu opens when the editor starts (and from RAC MAKER · Menu at the top left). It shows every map in your folder as a card with its picture. Click a card for Load, Play, Duplicate, Rename and Delete (deleted maps go to the Deleted folder), its named versions and its backups. Double-click a card to load it.
  • Save version (main menu, on the open map) keeps a named copy that is never pruned, unlike backups.
  • The camera button (top bar): fly the camera to the view you like and press it. The game view, without the panels, becomes the map's picture in the main menu.
  • The circle arrow next to it (on by default): every save takes the picture again from that spot, so it shows your latest edits. Maps made before this need the camera button pressed once.
